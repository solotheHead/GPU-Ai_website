RTX PRO 6000 vs H200 NVL: which GPU for your AI server?

The NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition and the H200 NVL are the two PCIe GPUs most often shortlisted for serious on-premises AI servers. One offers 96GB and Blackwell features at a lower price; the other offers 141GB of HBM3e and three times the memory bandwidth. This guide compares them on the numbers that decide real deployments.

The short answer

  • Choose the RTX PRO 6000 for most inference, fine-tuning, rendering and mixed workloads where 96GB per GPU is enough, especially if you want FP4 or need graphics capabilities. At our indicative prices it costs about 40% of an H200 NVL per card.
  • Choose the H200 NVL when memory bandwidth or capacity is the bottleneck: large models served to many users, long contexts, models split across GPUs with NVLink, or FP64 scientific computing.
  • Consider the RTX 5090 or 48GB RTX cards when the budget is tight and your models fit in 32–48GB per GPU.

Memory decides which models you can serve

When an LLM generates text, each new token requires reading the model weights from GPU memory. Memory capacity therefore sets how large a model and context you can hold, and memory bandwidth sets the ceiling on generation speed.

  • A 70B-parameter model in 8-bit needs about 70GB for the weights alone. It fits on one RTX PRO 6000 with some room for context, or on one H200 NVL with much more room for long contexts and concurrent users.
  • The H200 NVL’s roughly three times higher bandwidth gives much faster token generation when a model is memory-bound.
  • Blackwell’s FP4 support lets the RTX PRO 6000 run 4-bit models with hardware acceleration, halving the memory per parameter compared with FP8 — a practical way to fit larger models into 96GB.

Eight-GPU servers

Both cards are dual-slot, passively cooled and draw up to 600W, so the GPUs in an 8-GPU server alone can draw about 4.8kW, before processors, memory and storage. Plan power, cooling and colocation capacity before you choose.

Which one to buy for your project?

Private LLM inference for a team

RTX PRO 6000. 96GB per GPU covers most open models in 8-bit or 4-bit, at a much lower cost per GPU.

High-traffic LLM serving

H200 NVL, when throughput per GPU, long contexts or very large models matter more than hardware cost.

Fine-tuning and research

RTX PRO 6000 for most LoRA and medium-scale training; H200 NVL with NVLink for larger models split across GPUs.

Rendering, simulation and graphics

RTX PRO 6000, which has RT cores and video engines. The H200 NVL suits FP64 scientific computing instead.

Frequently asked questions

Is the RTX PRO 6000 Workstation Edition the same as the Server Edition?

Both use the same Blackwell GPU with 96GB of GDDR7. The Server Edition is passively cooled and relies on the server’s front-to-back airflow; the Workstation Edition has its own fans for desktop workstations. Our configurator lists the Server Edition at $11,900 and the Workstation Edition at $12,400 (indicative).

Does the H200 NVL work in any server?

No. It needs a server designed for passive 600W GPUs with sufficient airflow and power, and NVLink bridges need matching slot spacing. We confirm this for your platform in the quotation.

What about the H100 NVL or B200?

The H100 NVL has less memory (94GB) than the H200 NVL. B200 systems are SXM-based servers built around a GPU baseboard rather than PCIe cards — a different class of system. Contact us if your project needs that scale.
