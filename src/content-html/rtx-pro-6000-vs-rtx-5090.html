RTX PRO 6000 vs RTX 5090: which GPU for AI work?

Both are NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs with GDDR7 on a 512-bit interface and fifth-generation Tensor Cores. For AI the deciding difference is memory: 96GB on the RTX PRO 6000 against 32GB on the RTX 5090. This guide compares the two on specifications, cost per GB of GPU memory, power and the models each can run, so you can choose for your workload rather than by the price of a single card.

The short answer

  • Choose the RTX 5090 when your models fit in 32GB per GPU — for example 8B to 14B language models or image generation — and you want the lowest price per card.
  • Choose the RTX PRO 6000 when memory is the limit: 70B models in 8-bit, long contexts, several users, fine-tuning, or when you need ECC memory and Multi-Instance GPU. At our indicative prices it is also cheaper per GB of GPU memory.

The same amount of memory, compared

A single RTX 5090 is much cheaper than a single RTX PRO 6000. Compare them at the same total GPU memory and the picture changes:

96GB 1 card, $11,900, up to 600W 3 cards, $14,070, up to 1,725W
192GB 2 cards, $23,800, up to 1,200W 6 cards, $28,140, up to 3,450W

Power figures are NVIDIA’s maximum per card multiplied by the number of cards, for the GPUs only. In an eight-GPU server the difference is the total memory: eight RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition cards give 768GB for $95,200, while eight RTX 5090s give 256GB for $37,520.

Which models fit

32GB — one RTX 5090

8B to 14B models in 8-bit or 16-bit; 32B-class models in 4-bit with a short context.

96GB — one RTX PRO 6000

70B models in 8-bit with room for context; 32B models in 16-bit; mixture-of-experts models of about 120B in 4-bit.

A model that does not fit on one card must be split across several. Neither GPU has NVLink, so split models exchange data over PCIe, which adds latency. One 96GB card avoids splitting where three 32GB cards would need it. For the sizing arithmetic, see how much GPU memory a local LLM server needs.

Which one fits your project?

Image generation and small models

RTX 5090. When the work fits in 32GB, you get Blackwell performance at a much lower price per card.

Many independent jobs

RTX 5090, one job per GPU. Eight cards in one server give eight separate GPUs for parallel work.

Private LLMs for a team

RTX PRO 6000. 70B models in 8-bit fit on one card, with room for several users’ context.

Fine-tuning

RTX PRO 6000. 96GB per GPU reduces the need to split training across cards.

Shared GPUs

RTX PRO 6000. Multi-Instance GPU divides one card into up to four isolated instances.

Something in between

Our modified RTX 4090 48GB ($4,000 indicative) gives 48GB per card.

Frequently asked questions

Is the RTX 5090 suitable for a server?

The RTX 5090 we offer is a custom 32GB dual-slot configuration with passive cooling, intended for a compatible server chassis with suitable forced airflow. The warranty is confirmed in your written quotation.

Can I mix RTX 5090 and RTX PRO 6000 cards in one server?

Technically yes, but a model split across different GPUs is limited by the slowest card and the smallest memory. Identical GPUs are easier to run and to expand. We confirm the configuration in your quotation.

Is the RTX PRO 6000 faster than the RTX 5090?

On NVIDIA’s figures the RTX PRO 6000 has more CUDA cores and a higher AI TOPS figure, and both have 1,792 GB/s of memory bandwidth in the Workstation Edition and RTX 5090. The bigger practical difference for AI is that three times the memory lets larger models run on one card.
