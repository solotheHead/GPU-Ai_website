Buy or rent a GPU server? Where the break-even point is

Renting GPU capacity costs nothing up front; buying costs a lot once and little per hour. Which is cheaper depends on how many hours you will actually use the GPUs. This guide compares the purchase price of three GPUs we sell with the rental prices on the Vast.ai marketplace, works out the break-even point and lists what the numbers leave out.

The short answer

  • Rent for short projects, experiments and uneven demand. You pay only for the hours you use and can switch GPU models at any time.
  • Buy when the GPUs will be busy most of the time. Against the median rental prices below, a purchased GPU pays for itself after about 9–12 months of continuous use, or 19–23 months at half load.
  • Combine the two: own the capacity you use every day and rent extra GPUs for peaks.

What renting costs

Vast.ai is a marketplace where hosts set their own prices, so rates move with supply and demand. Its pricing page shows the lowest current offer and the 30-day median price per GPU per hour. On 12 November 2026 it showed:

GPU Lowest current offer 30-day median
RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (96GB) $1.07 per hour $1.67 per hour
H200 NVL (141GB) $3.60 per hour $4.39 per hour
RTX 5090 (32GB) $0.39 per hour $0.67 per hour

Prices are per GPU. Offers differ in reliability, location, network speed and the rest of the machine, and storage or data transfer can be charged on top, so check the offer itself. If you compare with another provider, use the price you would actually pay there: a higher rental price brings the break-even point below forward.

What owning costs

When you own the GPU, the main running cost per hour is electricity. NVIDIA rates the RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition and the H200 NVL at up to 600W and the RTX 5090 at 575W. At an example tariff of $0.20 per kWh:

GPU NodeX indicative price Maximum power Electricity per hour at full load
RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition $11,900 600W $0.12
H200 NVL $29,490 600W $0.12
RTX 5090 (passive, for servers) $4,690 575W about $0.115

The prices are for the GPU alone, from our configurator on 11 November 2026. The server around it — processors, memory, storage, chassis, power supplies — adds to the purchase price, and its fans and processors add to the power draw. We include all of that in the complete-server example below.

Break-even: when buying pays off

Every hour you use your own GPU instead of renting one saves the rental price minus your electricity cost. The purchase pays for itself after:

break-even hours = purchase price ÷ (rental price per hour − electricity per hour)

With the 30-day median rental prices and electricity at $0.20 per kWh (a month is about 730 hours):

GPU Break-even hours At 100% use At 50% use At 25% use
RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition about 7,680 10.5 months 21 months 42 months
H200 NVL about 6,910 9.5 months 19 months 38 months
RTX 5090 about 8,450 11.6 months 23 months 46 months

Two things move the result. The rental price matters most: against the lowest current offers instead of the median, full-use break-even takes about 17 months for the RTX PRO 6000, 12 months for the H200 NVL and 23 months for the RTX 5090. Electricity matters much less: at $0.30 per kWh, the RTX PRO 6000 breaks even after about 10.9 months of full use instead of 10.5.

A complete 8-GPU server

A GPU needs a server. Our reference 8× RTX PRO 6000 server costs from $121,384 indicative, including eight GPUs, processors, memory, storage, networking and a 3-year commercial hardware warranty. It needs more than 5kW at full load.

Renting eight RTX PRO 6000 GPUs at the median price costs $13.36 per hour. With the server drawing 5kW at $0.20 per kWh ($1.00 per hour), owning it saves about $12.36 per hour of use, so the complete server pays for itself after about 9,820 hours: about 13.5 months at full use, 27 months at half load or 54 months at a quarter. Colocation or your own premises, internet and maintenance come on top and lengthen these periods.

What the numbers leave out

  • Your data stays with you. On your own server, models, prompts and datasets never leave hardware you control, which can settle compliance questions that make cloud use difficult.
  • Availability. Marketplace capacity depends on what hosts offer at the moment. Your own GPUs are there when you need them, at a known cost.
  • Work and responsibility. Owning means installation, monitoring, repairs and a place with enough power and cooling. Renting leaves that to the host. A managed service can take this work over for an agreed fee.
  • Resale value. A purchased GPU keeps a resale value; rental payments do not. Newer models do push prices down over time, so plan for a realistic useful life.
  • Price changes. Rental prices for a GPU model usually fall as newer models arrive, which lengthens the break-even period of a purchase made today.

Owning and renting out idle time

If your own GPUs are idle part of the time, you can offer that capacity to others on a marketplace such as Vast.ai and use the income to shorten the payback. The host receives the rental price minus the platform fee, demand changes, and the machine has to meet the platform’s reliability requirements, so this income is variable and not guaranteed. How to decide

Rent if

the project is short or experimental, the workload is uneven, you are still choosing the GPU model, or you need many GPUs for a few days.

Buy if

the GPUs will run most of the time for a year or more, the data must stay on your hardware, or you want a fixed cost and guaranteed availability.

Combine if

you have a steady base load and occasional peaks: own the base, rent the peaks, and consider renting out what you do not use.

Frequently asked questions

Is renting always cheaper for a short project?

Usually. If you need GPUs for weeks rather than years, the rental cost stays far below the purchase price. The table above shows how many hours of use it takes for a purchase to pay off.

Why compare with Vast.ai and not a large cloud provider?

Vast.ai is a price-driven marketplace, which makes it a strict comparison for buying. If the provider you would actually use charges more per GPU-hour, a purchase breaks even sooner. Use the formula above with that provider’s current price.

Does the comparison include VAT?

The NodeX prices are indicative; VAT, delivery and availability are confirmed in your written quotation. Check how your rental provider charges VAT as well, so you compare like with like.

Can I rent out my server when I do not need it?

Yes, on platforms such as Vast.ai, if the machine meets their requirements. Income depends on demand, your prices and the platform fee, and is not guaranteed.
