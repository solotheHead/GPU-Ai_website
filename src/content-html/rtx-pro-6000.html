RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell: price, editions and specifications

The NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell is a professional GPU with 96GB of GDDR7 memory and fifth-generation Tensor Cores with FP4 support. It comes in three editions — Server, Workstation and Max-Q — that share the same memory but differ in cooling, power and where they belong. This page compares the editions, lists the specifications, shows our indicative EU prices and explains when an RTX 5090 or an H200 NVL is the better choice.

Which edition do you need?

  • Server Edition — for rack servers. It has no fans of its own and relies on the server’s front-to-back airflow, which is how our 4U GPU servers are built. Choose it for 2 to 8 GPUs in one chassis.
  • Workstation Edition — for a desktop workstation with one or two GPUs. Its fans push air through the card, which suits an open workstation case rather than a dense server.
  • Max-Q Workstation Edition — for workstations that need several GPUs on a limited power budget. At 300W it gives up some performance for half the power.

Whichever edition you choose, we confirm compatibility with the chassis, power supplies and cooling in your quotation.

The RTX PRO 6000 has no NVLink: several cards in one server communicate over PCIe. This works well for inference and for running several models side by side; models split across many GPUs gain more from NVLink, which the H200 NVL offers.

RTX PRO 6000 vs RTX 5090

Both are Blackwell GPUs with GDDR7 on a 512-bit interface. The difference that matters most for AI is memory: 96GB against 32GB. At our indicative prices the RTX 5090 server version costs $4,690 and the RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition $11,900 — about $147 against $124 per GB of GPU memory.

  • Choose the RTX 5090 when your models fit comfortably in 32GB per GPU — for example 8B to 14B models, or image generation — and the budget per GPU matters most.
  • Choose the RTX PRO 6000 when you need memory. One card holds what would otherwise be split across three RTX 5090s.

Specifications, cost at equal memory, power and use cases are in our full RTX PRO 6000 vs RTX 5090 comparison.

What 96GB of GPU memory is good for

LLM inference

A 70B model in 8-bit needs about 70GB for the weights, so it fits on one card with room for context. A 32B model fits even in 16-bit.

Larger models across GPUs

Two to four cards give 192–384GB for 70B models in 16-bit or 120B-class models in 8-bit; eight cards give 768GB.

Fine-tuning

LoRA and medium-scale fine-tuning benefit from 96GB per GPU, which reduces the need to split work across cards.

Rendering and video

RT Cores and four encoders and decoders per card suit rendering, simulation and video pipelines.

Frequently asked questions

Is the RTX PRO 6000 the same as the RTX 6000 Ada?

No. The RTX 6000 Ada is the previous generation, with 48GB of GDDR6 and no FP4 support. The RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell has 96GB of GDDR7. Both are in our configurator: the RTX 6000 Ada at $6,726 and the RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition at $11,900 (indicative).

Can I put the Workstation Edition in a rack server?

The Workstation Edition is designed for the airflow of a desktop workstation, while the Server Edition is designed to be cooled by a server’s fans. For multi-GPU rack servers we recommend the Server Edition and confirm the final choice in your quotation.

Does the RTX PRO 6000 support NVLink?

No. Cards communicate over PCI Express Gen 5. If you need NVLink between GPUs, compare it with the H200 NVL.

Do you sell the Max-Q edition?

It is not in our configurator at the moment. Ask us in your request and we will check availability and price.
